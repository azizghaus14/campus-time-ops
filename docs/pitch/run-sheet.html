"Good morning. We're Team Terminal, and this is Campus Time Ops.

APU's timetable changes every single day.

Lecturers get sick: with 494 lecturers, around 20 times every teaching week. One sick day at 7 a.m. means 3 classes and about 500 students to re-plan before 8:30.

Rooms close for repairs: one busy room shut for a week leaves about 20 classes with nowhere to go.

Holidays get announced at short notice: in 2022, on a Thursday, for the next Monday. At APU, one Monday is 598 classes.

And the everyday jobs: vivas that need three examiners and a room free at the same time. Make-up classes that must fit every group's timetable. Presentations and events. Students looking for a free room to study, or asking "how many more classes can I miss?"

Today, all of it is done by hand. We built AI agents that do it in seconds, with a human approving every step."

Speaker: say this from memory, looking at the judges. Pace: the first three (sick, room, holiday) slowly with their numbers; the everyday list faster, like a list. One number, small pause, next number. Pause for one second at the end.
"Around 20 times a week" = 494 lecturers × 2 sick days a year ÷ about 45 teaching weeks ≈ 22 (an assumption, not APU data). "About 20 classes" = our benchmark: the 5 busiest rooms closed Mon–Fri hit 101 classes, about 20 each. 2022: on Thu 24 Nov the new Prime Minister declared Mon 28 Nov a public holiday (The Star, Bernama). Vivas, make-ups, presentations, events: Slot Finder. Free rooms and "how many more can I miss": student view. All are real features. "About 500 students" uses estimated group sizes: the public feed doesn't give class sizes.

7 · How it works

Slide: Architecture

"Three pieces make this work.

One: Gemini. The core actions you saw run on an instant, predictable path. Any open question goes to Gemini, which uses read-only tools on the live timetable. And our Replan and Slot Finder agents are built for Gemini Enterprise, on the same tools.

Two: an MCP server. MCP is the open standard that gives AI agents real tools. Ours has 28 tools: read the timetable, find substitutes, find rooms, draft emails.

Three: Google's OR-Tools CP-SAT solver. A chatbot can talk about a timetable, but it can't guarantee zero clashes across 600 classes. A solver can.

The AI understands, the solver plans, the human decides."

Don't say users can chat with it in Gemini Enterprise yet: the agent is registered there, but the hackathon project has no Google sign-in set up, so nobody can open the Gemini Enterprise chat. Say "registered in Gemini Enterprise" or "built for Gemini Enterprise". If Gemini was off in the check, the speaker drops the Gemini sentence and says: "Our Replan and Slot Finder agents are built for Gemini Enterprise, on these same tools."

Judge questions: short answers

What if the AI makes a mistake?It can't act without approval, and a separate clash checker verifies every plan before anyone sees it. You can also adjust it in plain English.
Is this real APU data?Yes. APU's public timetable feed, with staff names pseudonymised. The students are synthetic, so no personal data is used.
Why not just use ChatGPT or Gemini?A language model can't guarantee zero clashes across 600 classes. We use AI to understand people and a real optimisation solver for the scheduling.
Is the chat using Gemini?For open questions, yes: that's the "Answered by Gemini" label, with read-only tools. The core actions (sick day, holiday, approve) use a fast rules path, so they're instant, work offline and give the same result every time. The planning itself is done by OR-Tools, not by a language model.
Where does Gemini Enterprise fit?Our Replan and Slot Finder agents are configured for Gemini Enterprise and use our MCP server as their tools, with Google sign-in. Our agent is registered in the hackathon's Gemini Enterprise app as an A2A agent and answers live (it drafts the MZD plan in about 9 s). Users can't open it yet only because the project's Google sign-in (identity provider) isn't set up; that's an admin setting.
Why can't Gemini change the timetable?On purpose. Changes go through a plan that a person approves. Gemini only gets tools to look things up, and it tells users the exact sentence to start a change.
How would APU adopt it?Keep the same tools layer, swap the public feed for the APSpace API, add APU sign-in, and pilot it with one school.
What does it cost to run?One small server. The solver (Google OR-Tools) is free and open source.
Does it send emails by itself? / Can we see an email?No. It only creates drafts after approval. A person presses send. Operator: jump to slide 10, which shows two real drafts from the sick-day demo.
Where do "an hour" and "200 hours" come from?Our estimate of about 20 minutes per class by hand: find a slot, check clashes, book a room, email everyone. 3 classes is about 1 hour; 598 classes is about 200 hours. We'd confirm the real figure with APU's timetabling office.
Are the students real?No. The 3,005 students are simulated on APU's real timetable, so no personal data is used. The "1 in 7 below 80%" comes from that simulation.

Three things to remember

  1. Say the tagline three times: in the hook, the solution and the close. "With a human approving every step."
  2. When a result appears on screen, the speaker stops talking for one second so the judges can see it.
  3. Rehearse together twice with a timer, using the real tabs, before the pitch.
